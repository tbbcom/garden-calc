Quick answer

Plan your vegetable garden around your average last spring frost date. Start tomatoes and peppers indoors 6–10 weeks before it, plant cool-season crops like peas, spinach and lettuce outdoors 2–6 weeks before it, and wait until 1–3 weeks after it to set out tomatoes, beans, squash and other warm-season crops. How this planting calendar works

The calendar looks up your USDA plant hardiness zone from your ZIP code, using the 2023 USDA map, and your average last spring and first fall frost dates from the nearest NOAA weather station (1991–2020 climate normals). See every station's dates on our frost dates by ZIP code tool. Every planting window is then counted in weeks from those two dates, the same method university extension services use in their regional planting guides.

Zones are based on winter low temperatures, not frost dates, so two places in the same zone can have frost dates a couple of weeks apart. If your local extension office or weather service publishes average frost dates for your town, type them into the two date boxes and every date in the table updates instantly.

When to plant tomatoes

Tomatoes are killed by frost and stall in cold soil, so timing matters more than for almost any other crop. Start seeds indoors 6–8 weeks before your last frost, and transplant outside 1–3 weeks after it, once nights stay above 50°F and the soil is at least 60°F. In zone 6 that usually means starting seeds in mid to late February and planting out in late April to early May. In zone 8, start in January and plant out from late March.

Average frost date ≠ last possible frost. An average last frost date means there's still roughly a 50% chance of a later frost. Keep a frost cloth or old bedsheet handy for tender transplants for two weeks after your date, and check the 10-day forecast before planting out.

Planting calendar FAQ

How do I find my last frost date?

Enter your ZIP code above: the calendar fills in the median last frost date from the nearest NOAA weather station (1991–2020 climate normals). The frost dates by ZIP code tool also shows a safer 10%-risk date and compares the three nearest stations, which helps if your garden sits higher or lower than the station.

What is the difference between a hardiness zone and a frost date?

A hardiness zone is based on the average coldest winter temperature and tells you which perennials survive winter. A frost date is when the last spring or first fall frost typically happens, and it tells you when to plant annual vegetables.

Can I plant before my last frost date?

Yes, for hardy crops. Peas, spinach, lettuce, kale, radishes, onions and potatoes tolerate light frost and are planted 2–6 weeks before the last frost. Wait until after it for tomatoes, peppers, beans, cucumbers, squash and basil.

When should I start seeds indoors?

Most vegetables are started 4–10 weeks before your last frost: peppers and onions earliest (8–12 weeks), tomatoes and cabbage-family crops 6–10 weeks, and cucumbers, squash and melons only 3–4 weeks before, because they grow fast and dislike root disturbance.

When should I plant a fall garden?

Count back from your first fall frost. Broccoli and cabbage transplants go out 10–12 weeks before it, carrots and peas are sown 10–12 weeks before, and fast crops like lettuce, spinach and radishes 4–8 weeks before.

Does this calendar work outside the US?

The ZIP lookup is US-only, but the calendar works anywhere: pick the zone that matches your climate or simply type your own average last and first frost dates into the two date boxes.
